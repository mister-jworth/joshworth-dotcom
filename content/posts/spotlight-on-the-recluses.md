<section class="fb-section"><div class="fb-row"><div class="fb-col fb-col-100"><p>Nowadays, it's perfectly easy and acceptable to tell the world all about the intimate details of your life. With just a few thumb-swipes, you can upload a photo of your new bangs, post a review of your pajamas, or alert your followers when you've checked-in to the dermatologist's office. If you're not doing such things, people might start to think you're being anti-social, stand-offish, or even worse, an old fuddy-duddy.</p>
<p>But rather than stress too much about my infrequent blogging, tweeting, and facebooking, I thought I might take a moment to honor some notable people who somehow pulled off the trick of making a name for themselves while still maintaining private lives that were actually private.</p></div>
<div class="fb-col fb-col-100"><hr class="separator" />
</div>
</div>
</section>

</div>
<div class="fb-col fb-col-50"><h3>Emily Dickinson</h3><h5><i>1830-1886</i></h5><p>You won't find one selfie of this woman wearing a flower-crown at Coachella, yet she's revered as one of the greatest poets of the nineteenth century. And she did it all while hiding from the specter of death in a Massachusetts farmhouse.</p></div>
<div class="fb-col fb-col-100"><hr class="separator" />
</div>
</div>
</section>
<section class="fb-section"><div class="fb-row"><div class="fb-col fb-col-50"><figure class="imageframe"><img src="/uploads/2019/10/hughes-frame.jpg" alt="" loading="lazy" /></figure>
</div>
<div class="fb-col fb-col-50"><p><h3>Howard Hughes</h3>
<h5><i>1905-1976</i></h5>
In his later years, this business tycoon / film mogul / master aviator eschewed the talk-show circuit and, instead, closed the black-out curtains on a Las Vegas hotel room to spend more time growing his fingernails and hiding from germs.</p></div>
<div class="fb-col fb-col-100"><hr class="separator" />
</div>
</div>
</section>
<section class="fb-section"><div class="fb-row"><div class="fb-col fb-col-50"><figure class="imageframe"><img src="/uploads/2019/10/garbo-frame.jpg" alt="" loading="lazy" /></figure>
</div>
<div class="fb-col fb-col-50"><p><h3>Greta Garbo</h3>
<h5><i>1905-1990</i></h5>
While the other stars of the silver screen were granting interviews and showing-off their evening gowns on Oscar night, the mysterious Ms. Garbo sat at home donning a comfortable shroud of shadowy seclusion.</p></div>
<div class="fb-col fb-col-100"><hr class="separator" />
</div>
</div>
</section>
<section class="fb-section"><div class="fb-row"><div class="fb-col fb-col-50"><figure class="imageframe"><img src="/uploads/2019/10/salinger-frame.jpg" alt="" loading="lazy" /></figure>
</div>
<div class="fb-col fb-col-50"><p><h3>J.D. Salinger</h3>
<h5><i>1919-2010</i></h5>
When he wasn't busy penning classic literature for your high-school English syllabus, this guy was holed-up in Cornish, New Hampshire filing lawsuits against anyone who dared to make him the subject of their publication.</p></div>
<div class="fb-col fb-col-100"><hr class="separator" />
</div>
</div>
</section>
<section class="fb-section"><div class="fb-row"><div class="fb-col fb-col-50"><figure class="imageframe"><img src="/uploads/2019/10/ditko-frame.jpg" alt="" loading="lazy" /></figure>
</div>
<div class="fb-col fb-col-50"><p><h3>Steve Ditko</h3>
<h5><i>1927-2018</i></h5>
The comic artist who brought us mega-heroes like Spider-man and Dr. Strange let his co-creator Stan Lee do all the talking while he remained hunched over a drawing table, shielding his life from the evil gaze of diabolical fans and sinister interviewers. </p></div>
<div class="fb-col fb-col-100"><hr class="separator" />
</div>
</div>
</section>
<section class="fb-section"><div class="fb-row"><div class="fb-col fb-col-100"><p>In celebration of these, and all the other publicity-shy public figures, let us forgo the applause, nod politely, then turn away and honor them with a little bit of the peace and quiet they seemed to cherish.</p></div>
</div>
</section>
