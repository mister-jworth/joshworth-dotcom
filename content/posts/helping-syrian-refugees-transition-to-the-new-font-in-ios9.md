<p><em>Lately, in my various feeds, I've been reading a lot of stories about the Syrian refugee situation, as well as stories about Apple's new font. Headlines of both topics scroll past me with the same weight and importance. Here's what happens when I find myself concerned about such disparate topics:</em></p>
<hr />
<p>When faced with an immense human tragedy like the mass exodus of millions of Syrians from their war-ravaged homeland, many of us are simply paralyzed by our feelings of helplessness and inadequacy. Of course we can offer our <a href="https://donate.unrefugees.org/ea-action/action?ea.client.id=1873&amp;ea.campaign.id=41888&amp;ea.tracking.id=D15XSJ151XXC&amp;gclid=CjwKEAjwkK6wBRCcoK_tiOT-zFASJAC7RArim4UV9cdhjNXIcIKYMNgymB_BD-pBZ-4SLe2UW1lHExoCwMXw_wcB&amp;gclsrc=aw.ds" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">financial support</a>,<a href="https://www.justgiving.com/handinhandforsyria/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"> send food and clothing</a>, or <a href="http://www.reliefandreconciliation.org/volunteer-with-us.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">volunteer</a> in some way. But there is an important issue that these refugees face that few people have been brave enough to step forward and address. I'm speaking, of course, about the switch to a new default system font in Apple's iOS9.</p>
<p>I, for one, will not sit idly by as boatloads of my fellow humans find themselves confused and disoriented by this seemingly arbitrary typographic upheaval. That is why I have taken it upon myself to publish this brief guide to the new font, so that designers across the world can help usher the tired and hungry masses through this difficult transition. Let us tackle these tough issues head-on by answering a few of the questions that these weary asylum-seekers must be asking as they wait huddled in overcrowded, makeshift border camps with no plumbing, very little to eat, and unfamiliar numerals in the time display of the iPhone lock screen.
<h3>What exactly is going on?</h3>
While the situation is constantly changing and extremely complex, the simplest way to explain the current circumstances is that Apple devices are undergoing a dramatic switch from Helvetica Neue, the quintessential sans-serif, to a completely new, internally designed font known as San Francisco. An increased x-height, ascenders that are higher than the cap-height, and the introduction of automatic optical spacing have had a jarring impact on the balance of positive and negative space in the user interface. But even though all this may seem chaotic and irrational, we can find solace in the fact that it is still a sans-serif font with overall vertical metrics which are identical to the previous system font.
<h3>What led to this?</h3>
Understanding the historical context for such a cataclysm can help alleviate feelings of disorientation and alienation. Many observers have noted that this is Europe's largest influx of refugees since World War II, but little attention has been paid to the fact that it is also the first time in 20 years that Apple has used an internally designed font for its operating systems. Though there were signs that such an event was inevitable, nobody can be blamed if they failed to see it coming.</p>
<p>Nonetheless, this is just one of many seismic shifts that have occurred throughout Apple's tumultuous history. Since the release of the first Macintosh computer in 1984, Apple has been tinkering and re-inventing its approach to how fonts are handled. Let us not forget that it was not long ago that Lucida Grande replaced Charcoal which replaced Chicago before that.</p>
<p>With 20/20 hindsight, we can look back to the fall of 2014 when a compact sub-family of the San Francisco font appeared in the OS of the Apple Watch and see that it was actually the harbinger of a massive change. It made sense that this small wearable would be accompanied by a new typeface to help with legibility, but few people suspected it would lead to the complete overthrow of Helvetica Neue as the basis for Apple's typographic identity.
<h3>But why? Why is this happening?</h3>
Many refugees are asking themselves "Why?" "Why was my home destroyed? Why must my family suffer like this? Why do the flags of the lowercase r and f suddenly extend further when used below 20pt?" In times of turmoil, the answers to these questions do not come easily. But we mustn't forget that the basic precepts of optimized legibility and visual balance have been a part of Apple's typographic philosophy for decades and such ideas are integral to their very identity. When faced with an ever-growing need for a consistent typographic voice and reading experience across all its platforms, they really had no choice but to react with a font that would be clearly legible and stylistically consistent at different screen sizes, light conditions, viewing distances, and resolutions.
<h3>What does the future hold?</h3>
Now that the fully-realized San Francisco font has arrived in iOS9, we can expect to see it quickly overtake the entire Apple ecosystem, first with OS X El Capitan, then in the new tvOS.</p>
<p>With its two variants, and two sub-families with six weights for text purposes and nine for display, San Francisco is a font that the Syrian refugees should plan on living with for the foreseeable future.</p>
<p>Perhaps, in time, the promises of size-specific tracking, and support for both proportional and monospaced numbering will lead to unforeseen opportunities and, in time, a better life for the innocent civilians caught in the midst of this historic upheaval.</p>
<p>Until then, look toward the light, be bold, and hope that all is not as black and heavy as it seems.</p>
