<p>Many of my dreams take place in malls. Expansive, echoing spaces with marble floors and flat stone walls.</p>
<p>In recent years, thanks to the rise in online retail, and the recent lack of foot traffic due to COVID, these once-bustling epicenters of consumerism have begun to fade into a state of abandonment and disrepair that resembles the subconscious landscapes I find myself wandering through while sleeping.</p>
<p>This transformation has been well-documented by some talented photographers and journalists...</p>
<p>https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_mall</p>
<p>Seph Lawless</p>
<p>https://www.instagram.com/sephlawless/?hl=zh-cn</p>
<p>https://www.cnn.com/style/article/abandoned-malls-america-photos/index.html</p>
<p>https://www.businessinsider.com/american-retail-apocalypse-in-photos-2018-1#some-of-these-mall-closures-are-less-recent-dixie-square-mall-in-chicago-which-was-once-home-to-60-different-stores-including-jcpenney-and-walgreens-was-left-vacant-for-33-years-before-being-demolished-in-2012-49</p>
<p>https://www.killerurbex.com/dead-malls/</p>
<p>While these photos are gorgeous examples of urban decay, I'm a bit more intrigued by the ones that are still open. Existing in a half-awake, twilight zone between commerce and despair, where you can witness businesses walking the fine line between commercial existence and non-existence.</p>
<p>The ones where you can find a Hot Topic, Claire's or Annie's Pretzels separated by an entire hallway of gated-off storefronts.</p>
